An Open Letter

To All Americans whom tired of not knowing who's really on the other end of the line,

It's Time to put Trust of Digital World

Every day, millions of Americans pick up the phone, open an email, or send data or read a text message and have to ask themselves a question that never should have been necessary:

Is this really who it says it is? Scammers impersonating banks, government agencies, even public figures, have turned that uncertainty into a weapon.

In 2024 alone, internet crime cost Americans over $16 billion a year — much of it built on nothing more sophisticated than a fake identity behind a screen.

Senator John Thune authored the TRACED Act, the law that forced voice providers to adopt STIR/SHAKEN caller authentication and gave the FCC real enforcement teeth against spoofed calls.

Senator Ted Cruz, as a senior member of the Commerce Committee that oversaw that effort, has continued pushing for stronger anti-spoofing and anti-fraud protections in the years since.

And behind the scenes, the Industry Traceback Group — the private-sector consortium tasked with tracking illegal robocalls back to their source — has shown that cross-provider cooperation, not isolated platform-by-platform fixes, is what actually shuts bad actors down.

That model worked for phone calls. It's time to extend it everywhere.

That's why we're building Sender Passport — a universal sender identity verification framework for digital communication broadly, not just the phone network. Verified sender credentials, ISP-level enforcement at the network edge (enduser), and a shared cross-provider database modeled on the traceback and revocation approach that STIR/SHAKEN pioneered — so a bad actor gets cut off everywhere at once, not just on whichever platform happened to catch them first.

This is the next chapter of a fight Congress and Industry have already proven can be won. We just have to finish the job.

Here's how you can be part of it: Visit the project site to see the full framework — the architecture, the enforcement model, and the problem it's built to solve.
